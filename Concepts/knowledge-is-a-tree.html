Knowledge is a Tree

This is a work in progress, a very rough v0.1 draft

All knowledge is built brick by brick, putting together one concept on top of another

A better analogy is: Knowledge is a tree


To put it simply - in computer science, this is a tree:

Knowledge is made of one concept, connecting to another, which connects to another.

The main root leaves (1) are often axioms or as some people call them, "First Principles". (Musk is a famous proponent of this concept.) It is the starting assumption, from which other leaves are derived. Everything else grows from here.

Example:

In physics: “Energy is conserved.”
In logic: “A statement is either true or false (law of excluded middle).”
In geometry: “A straight line can be drawn between any two points.”
Everything else grows from here.

From these root leaves (1), we apply reasoning, operations, or observation to derive new leaves or new concepts (2, 3, 4 and so on..).


Schools often teach try to teach you these leafs (concepts), but fail to fully explain how one leaf is connected to another leaf, or why this leaf was branched out as a new one.

If you've ever thought "Why are they teaching me Trigonometry? This doesn't even make sense" - odds are that you don't understand the connection from triangles to trigonometry. How did the concept (leaf) of Triangles branch out to Trigonometry? Why did people even come up with this? How is it being used in real life? These questions need to be answered before a connection is formed in your head, and the leaf can now grow strongly.

If it doesn't make sense to you, that connection is lost, and the leaf is often stranded.

Leaves that are not connected to the tree fall down and are forgotten. It is possible to score highly on exams by learning these concepts, but not really connecting these to the knowledge tree that's present in your head. It may work for the exam, but they don't stick in your memory for the long run.


I've found that seeing all knowledge (even knowledge about myself) being represented as a tree - quickly tells me the gaps of if I truly understand something. This helps with deeper introspection of how the world work and more importantly how I work.
