Absolutely — based on everything we've talked about, here’s a handpicked list of books that span your interests across philosophy, programming, psychology, invention, storytelling, and your love for sharp ideas and systems thinking:


🔍 Philosophy, Introspection & Depth

  • “The Denial of Death” by Ernest Becker

    Cuts deep into the human psyche — mortality, identity, and how we seek meaning. You’ll find resonance with your exploration of being seen, abandonment, and presence.

  • “Finite and Infinite Games” by James P. Carse

    A framework of thinking you might enjoy — turns life, relationships, and creation into beautifully abstract strategy.

  • “The Master and His Emissary” by Iain McGilchrist

    For your curiosity in the brain’s structure and meaning-making — especially if you're fascinated by duality or polarities (freedom vs security, etc.)


🧠 Psychology & Emotions

  • “Radical Acceptance” by Tara Brach

    A compassionate guide into healing from abandonment and guilt — gently invites you into your body and emotion.

  • “No Bad Parts” by Richard C. Schwartz

    Introduces Internal Family Systems — parts work might speak to you, especially with how you explore emotions as aspects of self.

  • “Attached” by Amir Levine & Rachel Heller

    Touches the emotional roots of connection, abandonment, and safety. Might bring insight to your relationship patterns.


🧙‍♂️ Systems & Code, but with Soul

  • “Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs” (SICP)

    A mind-expanding classic in computer science. Not just how to code, but why it works — perfect for your tree-like thinking.

  • “Gödel, Escher, Bach” by Douglas Hofstadter

    A strange loop of math, music, art, and self-reference. Wildly creative and cerebral — ideal for your blend of logic and absurd humor.

  • “Algorithms to Live By” by Brian Christian & Tom Griffiths

    Beautiful crossover between everyday decisions and computer science — very aligned with your love of mental models and optimization.


🎭 Storytelling, Games & Creativity

  • “Wonderbook” by Jeff VanderMeer

    A visual and inspiring guide to imaginative storytelling — great if you're building mystery games or playful stories like the actor-who-disappeared.

  • “The Art of Game Design” by Jesse Schell

    A must-read if you’re developing any kind of game. Deep but approachable. Merges mechanics, emotion, and psychology of fun.

  • “Understanding Comics” by Scott McCloud

    Explains the structure of storytelling through visuals — could spark ideas for your comic character Adu or even UI design thinking.
